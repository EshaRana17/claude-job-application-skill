# Cover Letter Guide

A cover letter answers three questions the resume cannot: why this company, why this role, and which two pieces of evidence matter most. Keep it short, specific and true.

## 1. Length and format

- 250-350 words in the body. Report the word count with the draft.
- Four paragraphs, three to five sentences each.
- Same font and header as the resume if it is a separate document.
- Address a named person if the user knows one: "Dear Ms. Rivera" or "Dear Sam Rivera". Otherwise "Dear Hiring Manager" or "Dear [Team name] team". Avoid "To whom it may concern".
- If the portal has a plain-text box, drop the letter header and keep only the greeting, body and sign-off.

## 2. Structure

| Paragraph | Purpose | Approximate words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Hook | Specific reason for writing, the role name, and a one-line thesis of fit | 50-70 |
| 2. Proof story 1 | Evidence for the most important must-have | 70-90 |
| 3. Proof story 2 | Evidence for the second must-have or a key differentiator, plus "why us" | 70-90 |
| 4. Close | Summary of value, call to action, thanks | 40-60 |

## 3. Writing the hook

The first sentence should be impossible to reuse for a different company. Build it from one of these sources, using only facts from the posting or the user:

- **A problem in the posting**: "Your posting says the data team is rebuilding reporting so that store managers can act on it the same day. I did that for 140 retail locations at Northwind."
- **A product or service the user knows first-hand**: "I have used [product] to run my team's sprint planning for two years, so the chance to work on its reporting features stands out."
- **A mission or customer group the user has a real link to**: "I spent four years as a hospital pharmacist before moving into software, so a role building tools for pharmacy teams is a direct fit."
- **A referral**: "[Name], who works on your platform team, suggested I apply." Only if it is true and the referrer agreed.

If the user cannot give any specific connection, use the most distinctive requirement in the posting as the hook. Do not invent enthusiasm for news, funding or awards you cannot verify.

## 4. Proof stories

Each story maps to one named requirement. Use a compressed STAR shape:

1. **Context** in half a sentence.
2. **What the user did**, with the posting's language where accurate.
3. **Result**, with a real number or concrete outcome.
4. **Link back**: one clause tying it to this role.

Example shape (illustrative facts only):

> You need someone who can bring order to vendor onboarding. At Brightline Logistics I took over a supplier process that averaged 21 days and depended on email chains. I built a shared intake form and approval checklist with procurement and legal, which cut onboarding to 8 days within two quarters. I would bring the same approach to scaling your marketplace supplier base.

Do not repeat resume bullets word for word. Add the "how" and the "why" the resume has no room for.

## 5. The "why us" line

One or two sentences, grounded in something checkable: the product, a stated value or goal in the posting, a market the company serves, or the user's personal experience as a customer. If the user wants to mention recent company news, they must supply the source, and the draft should flag it as "verify before sending".

## 6. The close

- Restate value in one sentence, not a summary of the whole letter.
- Ask for the next step directly: "I would welcome the chance to talk about how I could help your team reach [goal from posting]."
- Thank them briefly.
- Sign-off: "Best regards," or "Sincerely," then full name. Add phone and email below if the letter is a standalone file.

## 7. Banned phrases and cliches

Check every draft against this list. Replace any match with a specific statement.

- "I am writing to apply for..." as the opening sentence
- "I believe I would be a great fit" / "perfect fit" / "ideal candidate"
- "I am passionate about..." without evidence of the passion
- "Dynamic", "results-driven", "self-starter", "go-getter", "rockstar", "ninja", "guru"
- "Team player", "hard worker", "detail-oriented" as standalone claims
- "Think outside the box", "hit the ground running", "wear many hats"
- "Leverage my skills", "synergy", "value-add"
- "Proven track record" without the record itself
- "To whom it may concern"
- "Thank you for your time and consideration" as the only closing line
- "Although I lack..." or any apology for missing qualifications
- "I know I don't have X, but..."
- Repeating the company name more than three times
- Starting more than two sentences in a row with "I"

## 8. Special cases

- **Career changer**: lead with the transferable result most relevant to the new field, then name the specific steps taken to close the gap (course, project, certification in progress). Do not apologize.
- **Employment gap**: do not mention it unless the user wants to. If they do, one neutral sentence is enough ("After a planned year caring for a family member, I completed [course] and..."). Do not share health or personal details beyond what the user approves.
- **Internal application**: lead with what the user has delivered for the company and why the new team needs it. Keep it shorter (200-250 words).
- **Overqualified**: explain the deliberate choice in one positive sentence (scope, domain, location, balance), then prove fit.
- **No cover letter requested**: suggest a 3-4 sentence version for the "additional information" box or the recruiter email instead.

## 9. Final checks

- [ ] Word count is 250-350
- [ ] Hook is specific to this company
- [ ] Two proof stories, each linked to a named requirement
- [ ] Every fact and number came from the user
- [ ] No banned phrases
- [ ] Company name and job title spelled exactly as in the posting
- [ ] No sensitive personal data
- [ ] Placeholders listed at the end for the user to fill
