# Outreach and Follow-up Guide

Short, specific messages get replies. Long, generic ones get archived. This file covers LinkedIn connection notes, cold emails, referral asks, and follow-up timing with templates.

## 1. General rules

- One clear ask per message.
- Mention the exact role title and, if available, the job ID.
- Lead with what is relevant to them, not with the user's life story.
- Never claim a shared connection, a past conversation, or a referral that did not happen.
- Never send to anyone on the user's behalf. Provide drafts; the user sends them.
- Use `[Name]`, `[Company]`, `[Role]` placeholders for anything the user has not given.
- No attachments in a first cold message unless the posting or contact asks for them. Offer a link instead.
- Respect a "no" or silence after the follow-up limit below.

## 2. LinkedIn connection note (under 300 characters)

LinkedIn caps connection notes at 300 characters for most accounts (some free accounts get fewer personalized notes per month, so make each one count). Always count characters including spaces and report the count.

Formula: who you are in a few words + specific reason + light ask.

Template A (recruiter):

```
Hi [Name], I applied for the [Role] role at [Company]. I have [N] years in [core skill from posting], most recently [one-line result]. Would be glad to connect and answer any questions about my background.
```

Template B (someone on the team):

```
Hi [Name], I'm a [title] interested in [Company]'s [team or product]. Your work on [specific thing they posted or built] caught my eye. I'm applying for [Role] and would value connecting.
```

Template C (alum or shared community):

```
Hi [Name], fellow [school or group] here. I'm exploring [Role] at [Company] and would appreciate connecting. No pressure at all.
```

Only use Template B's "your work on" line if the user names something real that the person did.

## 3. Cold email to a recruiter or hiring manager

- Subject line: specific, under about 60 characters. Examples: `[Role] application - [Name], [top credential]` or `Question about [Role] (Job ID 12345)`.
- Body: 90-150 words, three short paragraphs.

Template:

```
Subject: [Role] application - [Full Name], [top relevant credential]

Hi [Name],

I applied for the [Role] position [on date / today] and wanted to introduce myself briefly. [One sentence: who the user is and their most relevant strength.]

[One or two sentences: a specific result that maps to the top must-have, with a real number.] [One sentence: why this company or team, grounded in the posting or something the user knows.]

Would you be open to a 15-minute call, or could you point me to the right person on the team? My resume is attached [or: linked here: URL].

Thank you,
[Full Name]
[Phone] | [LinkedIn URL]
```

## 4. Referral ask

Make it easy to say yes and easy to say no. Adjust the warmth to the actual relationship.

Warm contact (former colleague, friend):

```
Hi [Name],

I hope [something specific and true about them] is going well. I noticed [Company] is hiring a [Role] ([link]) and it lines up closely with my work on [relevant area].

Would you be comfortable referring me? If yes, I've added a short blurb below you can forward, and I'm happy to send my resume. If it's not a good fit or the timing is wrong, no problem at all.

Thanks either way,
[Name]
```

Weak tie (met once, alum, second-degree connection):

```
Hi [Name],

We [met at X / both studied at Y]. I'm applying for the [Role] role on your [team] team ([link]). Before I apply, could I ask you two or three quick questions about the team, over message or a 15-minute call?
```

For weak ties, ask for information first, not a referral. A referral can come later if the conversation goes well.

## 5. Forwardable blurb

Include this with every referral ask. Three to four sentences, written in the third person so the contact can paste it:

```
[Full Name] is a [title] with [N] years in [area]. At [Company], [he/she/they] [one specific result with a real number]. [He/She/They] is applying for [Role] (Job ID [ID]) and brings [two must-have skills from the posting]. Resume: [link].
```

Ask the user which pronouns to use.

## 6. Follow-up timing table

Business days exclude weekends and public holidays.

| Situation | When to follow up | Maximum follow-ups |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Applied online, no response | 7-10 business days after applying | 1, then mark "No response" after 3-4 weeks |
| Cold email or LinkedIn message, no reply | 5-7 business days | 1 |
| After any interview | Thank-you note within 24 hours | 1 |
| Waiting for a decision after interview | 1-2 business days after the date they gave; if no date, 7 business days | 2, about a week apart |
| Referral ask, no reply | 5-7 business days | 1 |
| Offer received, need time | Reply within 24-48 hours to acknowledge and confirm the deadline | As needed |
| Rejected | Optional short note within a few days to stay in touch | 1 |

Add each due date to the `next_follow_up` column in `assets/application_tracker.csv`.

## 7. Follow-up templates

**After applying, no response**

```
Subject: Following up - [Role] application

Hi [Name],

I applied for the [Role] position on [date] and wanted to check whether there is any update on the timeline. I remain very interested, especially in [one specific aspect of the role]. Happy to send anything else that would help.

Best regards,
[Full Name]
```

**Thank-you after interview (within 24 hours)**

```
Subject: Thank you - [Role] interview

Hi [Name],

Thank you for your time today. I enjoyed discussing [specific topic from the conversation], and it made me more excited about [specific part of the role]. [Optional: one sentence adding something you forgot to say, or a short answer you want to improve.]

I look forward to hearing about next steps.

Best regards,
[Full Name]
```

Send a separate, slightly different note to each interviewer. Ask the user for one detail from each conversation.

**Checking on a decision**

```
Subject: [Role] - checking in

Hi [Name],

I hope your week is going well. I wanted to check in on the [Role] position, since you mentioned a decision around [date]. I'm still very interested and happy to provide anything else you need.

Best regards,
[Full Name]
```

**Acknowledging an offer and confirming the deadline**

```
Subject: Re: Offer - [Role]

Hi [Name],

Thank you very much for the offer. I'm excited about the opportunity. Could you confirm the date you need my decision by? I'd also appreciate a copy of the full written offer, including benefits, so I can review everything properly.

Best regards,
[Full Name]
```

**After a rejection**

```
Subject: Thank you - [Role]

Hi [Name],

Thank you for letting me know, and for the time you and the team spent with me. I enjoyed learning about [specific thing]. If you are able to share any feedback, I would welcome it. I'd be glad to be considered for future roles at [Company].

Best regards,
[Full Name]
```
